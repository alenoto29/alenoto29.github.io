04 / EXPORTS → SHARED VIEW

Paid acquisition reporting

I built a daily acquisition dashboard that combines platform exports with attribution data while preserving source differences. The app team uses it for monitoring; ingestion still depends on manually downloaded exports.

MATURITY

Team dashboard

SUPPORTED OUTCOME

Used by the app team

CONTRIBUTION

Built and analysed with AI assistance

The story

A new app project needed a shared view of acquisition performance. Spend lived in advertising platforms, while installs, events and revenue were spread across other exports. Bringing cost data into the attribution tool required a tier the team did not have. My manager and I agreed to build the missing view, and I implemented it with AI assistance.

The main design problem was deciding what each source could tell us. Platform-reported figures differed from attributed figures for the same dates. I designated native platform exports as the source for paid spend and delivery, while retaining a separately labelled attribution view. Raw source labels remain unchanged; readable display names are derived from them.

The Python pipeline ingests exports, normalises them into daily records by platform and channel, computes metrics, and renders a self-contained interactive page. It is deterministic: AI helped me build the code, but no language model runs inside the reporting pipeline. The dashboard is published behind access control.

I made missing-data states explicit. A missing export, a day without activity and a recorded zero remain distinguishable. Calculations with a zero denominator return an unavailable value. Inferred joins and timezone mismatches carry flags, and related campaign files are checked for matching totals before they are accepted.

These rules make the view easier to interpret without implying that the sources have become equivalent. The main daily metrics follow activity dates and do not establish cohort profitability, incrementality or causality. I still download the source exports manually, so updates are not unattended.

The dashboard is used across the app team, from leadership to interns, and leadership has requested additional metrics. I built it and keep it updated. Its demonstrated role is shared monitoring: I cannot identify a specific decision that changed because of it, and I have no measured time saving to claim.
