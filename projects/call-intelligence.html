Call intelligence

Why people call—and what happens next

Not every call starts with an intention to buy. Understanding that difference became the starting point for rethinking the phone menu's priorities.

From analysis to changes

Some phone-menu options brought in calls with little chance of converting. I designed the analysis around three connected steps: classify intent from the call opening, link it to recorded conversion outcomes, and examine unsuccessful calls for barriers. I guided Claude through each step and checked a sample against the transcripts.

Based on those findings, I proposed changes to the menu priorities. My manager approved them, and the team made some options less prominent and trained operators on the revised priorities.

Following these changes, conversion for the calls we studied reached its highest rate of the year so far, outside peak season. This was a before-and-after comparison: the menu changes, training and seasonality could all have influenced the result.

How the workflow works

I guided the original analysis in Claude:

  1. Read the opening. Exclude unreadable transcripts and classify why each person called using the opening only. This keeps an operator’s later sales pitch from being mistaken for the caller’s original intent.
  2. Connect intent to outcome. Match each call’s reason with its recorded conversion outcome, then compare conversion across reasons for calling.
  3. Look at what went wrong. For calls that didn’t convert, examine the rest of the conversation for barriers such as missing documents, eligibility or a need the service couldn’t meet.
  4. Check and act. Review a sample against the transcripts and turn the patterns into proposed menu priorities and operator guidance.

I later built a reusable local version with Python to prepare the inputs, check Claude’s structured responses and calculate rates from reviewed labels. That version is separate from the original analysis behind these results.
