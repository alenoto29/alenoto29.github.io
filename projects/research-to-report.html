Research to outreach

A press report in 65% less time

From a topic to a story journalists want, and the right people to send it to.

What changed
One report took 65% less time than one made the old way. The first was picked up by leading national publications, with an average Domain Rating of 90.
What I did
Built the whole workflow from scratch with Claude, so colleagues can run it themselves.
Who uses it
The PR team adopted it. They pick the angle and approve the draft.

The problem

The team needed two things at once: a report a newsroom would care about, and a reason to send it to one particular journalist.

The rule

Work out the numbers with code, then write around them. If the text asks for a number that doesn't exist, the workflow stops.

How it works

  1. Find sourcesA script searches three public research libraries, removes doubles and ranks what it finds.
  2. Choose what to exploreA first scan shows what's there. Colleagues choose what deserves a deeper look.
  3. CalculateCode and spreadsheets produce every figure and save it under a name.
  4. Write and checkText and charts use those names. A missing one stops the run. A person reviews the result.
  5. Prepare outreachThe report's angle guides which journalists to contact, and what to tell each one.

The honest bit

65% comes from comparing two reports, not an average over many. And a traceable number can still rest on a weak assumption, so the figures still need judgement.
