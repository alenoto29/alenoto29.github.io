03 / SIGNALS → FORECASTS

Electricity-price forecasting

I used AI assistance to build a daily electricity-price forecasting pipeline and separated its scheduled runs from my working copy. It remains a personal experiment, with no users and substantial modelling knowledge still to develop.

MATURITY

Experiment

SUPPORTED OUTCOME

Personal experiment; no users

CONTRIBUTION

Built and analysed with AI assistance

The story

I started this project to explore predictive modelling outside my marketing background. There was also a possible application: electricity-price forecasts could support a tool for estimating costs under a variable-price offer. That application was never built.

The system collects historical wholesale prices and external signals, prepares model inputs, and generates forecasts with uncertainty bands. A scheduled job updates a dashboard and maintains dated forecast snapshots. My contribution was initiating the project, working with AI to get it running end to end, and deciding to isolate the scheduled job from the checkout I use for editing.

That separation was a practical operational choice. The daily job runs from its own checkout, reducing the chance that unfinished edits interrupt its normal execution. It also means the working folder alone is insufficient to establish the automation's current state.

The modelling and validation methods were recommended by AI. I approved the proposed design, but my understanding of the statistical methods is still limited. I plan to study them before presenting this as evidence of deeper modelling competence. The running system demonstrates what I got working with assistance; it does not establish that I can independently design or validate its model.

The project records comparisons with a simple baseline, including rejected experiments, and has safeguards against overwriting historical forecasts. These controls also came from the AI-assisted design. They do not establish current forecast quality: the recorded run on 7 September 2026 produced a degraded output with a failing drift check. I was not actively monitoring it and did not intervene.

The result is an unattended forecasting experiment. Nobody currently uses its outputs, and it has no business outcome. The next learning task is to understand the methods and failure signals well enough to assess the estimates and decide when a run needs attention.

A CONVERSATION WORTH HAVING

Why I separated the scheduled checkout from my working copy, and what additional understanding I need to maintain the forecasting system.
