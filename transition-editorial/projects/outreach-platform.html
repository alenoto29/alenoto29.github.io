06 / Journalist outreach platform

Everything in one place

Chemu Ng’ok · Commune · 2026

From scattered sheets to a shared contact history.

I built a shared journalist database and dashboard with AI assistance, shaped by conversations with PR colleagues. It brought contacts, their history and summary KPIs into one place so the team could work faster.

Observed result

Scattered spreadsheets became one shared archive with faster contact lookup and a visible history of outreach KPIs.

The workflow improvement is qualitative; time saved and campaign lift have not been measured.

Bringing the team's knowledge together

Journalist and publisher information was spread across spreadsheets. The team needed a shared place to explore those contacts, find people relevant to their outreach and keep the information up to date.

I spoke with colleagues about how they selected journalists: which criteria mattered, how they prioritised them and what made someone unsuitable. Their working knowledge informed the system. I built the software with AI assistance, without a separate developer.

Built for people to contribute

The decision that shaped the project was shared editing. A colleague who found a useful contact needed to add it directly, so the information would be available to everyone else.

That requirement led me to build a shared backend database and browser dashboard. The database became the shared source, with forms for adding and editing records. The dashboard brought journalists, publishers and campaign information into one navigable workspace.

New-journalist discovery remained a separate workflow, with new records merged into the archive through deduplication. The report-specific research and pitch preparation belong to the Research to outreach workflow; this platform gives the team a shared contact base to work from.

A faster shared view

The platform replaced a search across scattered spreadsheets with one shared view. Colleagues could find a journalist, see prior contact and report history, and update the record for the next person. The dashboard also brought summary KPIs and outreach history together, instead of leaving them in separate files.

The practical result was quicker access to the information the team needed and a clearer record of its outreach work. The team contributed the knowledge behind the workflow and could maintain the contact base themselves. I have not measured the time saved or a change in campaign performance attributable to the platform.

Under the hood

A shared database. A Flask API connects the dashboard to SQLite. Soft deletion and a change log help preserve the history of edits, with continuous database replication to object storage.

Context around contacts. Supporting tools collect articles from author pages and connect journalists, publishers and topics. Inferred topics rely on keyword matching and need review; they are not verified statements about a journalist's interests.

Lightweight team access. Access is shared through a link and token distributed to selected people. It does not provide individual accounts or individual access revocation.
