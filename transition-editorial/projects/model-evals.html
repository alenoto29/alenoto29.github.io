Model evaluation

Chemu Ng’ok · Sensation IV · 2026

Finding the right model without overpaying

I built a repeatable evaluation suite around SEO tasks I actually do. It helped me choose lower-cost models that matched or exceeded the quality of more expensive options on those tasks, then reduce spending in my work.

Observed result

I used lower-cost models selected through the evaluation, reducing model spending while matching or improving quality on the tasks tested.

Saving is qualitative and user-reported; quality comparison is limited to the tested tasks and configurations.

How much model does the job need?

Using a frontier model for every task is an easy default, but repeated work makes cost and waiting time matter. A cheaper model can also produce a convincing answer while missing a link, changing a price or breaking a page component. I wanted to find where the less expensive option could still meet the requirements.

I chose five tasks from my SEO workflows and built the evaluation harness with AI assistance. The fixtures are synthetic, and each task has fixed checks for the output that matters: link relationships, evidence-based page findings, offer updates, constrained HTML edits and supplier calculations.

Putting quality beside resources

I checked the required outputs and model identity before including a run. The overall score is the equal-weight average of the five task scores. I compare output quality with each session's estimated cost and elapsed time. That makes the trade-offs visible without treating price or model reputation as a proxy for quality.

The chart includes only completed runs. Each model has one observation, so small differences could move on a repeat. The checks catch specified errors in facts, arithmetic and structure; they cannot fully assess how well a page reads.

What changed in my work

The highest-scoring run in this suite was not the most expensive, and a substantially cheaper model finished close behind it. For these tasks, better output did not simply follow higher spend or require the newest frontier model.

I then used the lower-cost models selected for suitable work, which reduced my model spending. The quality comparison comes from the tested tasks; I cannot quantify the saving as a percentage or claim the same performance for every future task.

Under the hood

One overall score. Five equally weighted task scores produce a result out of 100.

Recorded resources. Cost is a session estimate, while time and token counts come from the recorded run. None of them measures answer quality on its own.

A bounded comparison. The chart shows completed sessions for this suite. One run per model does not establish how reliably each result would repeat.
